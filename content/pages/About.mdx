      Global Writing Cultures is an open educational resource for the comparative study of premodern writing. It brings together object-based records and, where available, linked open data and open-access images so that students, researchers, and the public can explore historical languages, scripts, materials, formats, and practices across space and time.

      The project looks beyond the text alone. A written object is also a material thing made with particular tools, organized through particular layouts, and shaped by the communities and institutions that produced, read, displayed, stored, reused, and sometimes destroyed it.

      What is a writing culture?

      There is no single form of the premodern book, and no sharp boundary separates manuscripts from every other kind of written object. Writing appears on portable books and documents, monumental inscriptions, tablets, seals, textiles, coins, printed objects, and many other surfaces. GWC places these forms in conversation without treating the modern printed codex—or any one regional tradition—as the norm.

      What can you explore?

      GWC supports comparison at several scales. Visitors can investigate individual written objects, follow languages and scripts across regions, compare materials and formats, examine multilingual layouts, or situate objects within places, dates, religious traditions, and geopolitical contexts.

      The collection also opens larger questions:

      • How have writing and oral performance shaped one another?

      • How have states, empires, religious communities, and institutions used and supported writing?

      • Why do materials and book forms change within a region, and how do they travel between regions?

      • How do layout, ornament, images, and paratext organize information?

      • Why have some written objects survived while others were recycled, dispersed, or destroyed?

      • How do digital surrogates expand access while also changing what can be seen and studied?

      A comparative method

      GWC uses working cross-cultural typologies to make comparison possible across specialist traditions. Its categories describe such features as object type, material, format, content, script, multilingual layout, and historical context. These categories are preliminary rather than universal: they are revised when better evidence or scholarly review reveals that a distinction should be added, clarified, or removed.

      Short definitions and full field-by-field guidance are provided in Documentation, where they can be maintained as the data model evolves.

      Scope and limits

      GWC is a growing comparative collection, not a claim to reproduce the full surviving record of premodern writing. Survival is uneven. Materials decay at different rates; objects have been discarded, recycled, censored, excavated, collected, dispersed, and digitized under very different conditions. Museum and library digitization also varies greatly by place, institution, and tradition.

      Records therefore distinguish documented facts from approximations and unresolved questions wherever the underlying evidence permits. Each record should be read together with its cited sources and the rights statement supplied by the holding institution.

      Project history

      Work on Global Writing Cultures began in summer 2019. The project developed in conversation with the University of Iowa Mellon Sawyer Seminar, Cultural and Textual Exchanges: The Manuscript across Premodern Eurasia, and with the broader comparative questions that emerged from that seminar.
