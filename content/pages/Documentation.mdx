      Start here

      Search and filters

      Search by keyword, combine filters, clear a search, and move between a results list, map, and individual record.

      Maps and regional timelines

      Region boundaries, mapped places, and stacked geopolitical-context lanes are tools for comparison. They show broad geographic and historical relationships, but do not imply that modern borders existed in the past or that linguistic, political, religious, and cultural boundaries coincided.

      Reading a record

      An object description provides readable context. Structured fields support searching and comparison. Related authorities connect an object to shared entities such as languages, scripts, places, regions, and geopolitical contexts. Bibliographies document the evidence used for the record. External identifiers link to scholarly authorities. A digital-surrogate link leads to an online representation supplied by a holding institution or project. Rights statements govern reuse of images and other third-party material.

      Field guide

      The public fields are organized under these groups:

      • Identification and object type

      • Content and text type

      • Language and script

      • Material, format, structure, and joining

      • Layout and multilingual relationships

      • Decoration, paratext, and other physical features

      • Date and chronology

      • Place, region, and geopolitical context

      • Religious identity

      • Repository, shelfmark, and collection history

      • Sources, identifiers, and digital surrogates

      • Rights and reuse

      Working typologies

      GWC uses controlled terms to support comparison across specialist traditions. These are working cross-cultural typologies rather than universal or final classifications. They are revised when better evidence or scholarly review calls for a distinction to be added, clarified, or removed.

      Current typologies cover:

      • Object types

      • Content and text types

      • Materials

      • Formats and structural forms

      • Scripts—broad script identification only; paleographic styles belong elsewhere

      • Multilingual-object layouts

      • Religious identities

      • Regions and geopolitical contexts

      Evidence, approximation, and uncertainty

      Historical records often depend on incomplete or disputed evidence. GWC may use approximate date ranges, representative points, broad regional assignments, corpus-level dates, and provisional identifications when the evidence warrants them. These decisions should be labeled, and an approximation should not be read as an exact observation.

      Sources and linked data

      GWC records cite the sources used to identify and describe each object. Structured identifiers connect records, where available, to external scholarly authorities and holding institutions. A link is evidence and a route for further reading; it does not mean that GWC reproduces or endorses every statement on the linked site.

      Cite and reuse GWC

      Citations should identify the project or record, include its URL and access date, and include a record revision date or data-release version when available.

      Except where otherwise noted, original GWC text and data are licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: https\://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/. Images, digital surrogates, logos, trademarks, and other third-party material may carry different terms.

      Accessibility

      GWC aims to make its records and visualizations usable across devices and assistive technologies. Image descriptions, keyboard access, clear labels, readable color contrast, and alternatives to map-only information should be maintained as the site develops.

      Corrections and contributions

      GWC is a reviewed but evolving resource. When reporting an error or supplying stronger evidence, include the record URL, the field in question, and a citable source.

      Version history

      Significant changes to the schema, controlled vocabularies, search interface, regional model, and major data releases should be recorded with dates. Blog announcements may point to those notes, while Documentation remains the authoritative account of the current data model and interface.
